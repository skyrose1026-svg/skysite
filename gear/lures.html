Lures & Flies

Lures imitate prey, flash or vibrate to attract attention, or trigger a reaction strike. Choose a size and weight your rod can cast, and retrieve it at a depth and speed that fit the water.

Common choices

Spinners

A rotating blade sends flash and vibration through the water. Cast upstream or across current and retrieve steadily.

Good for: trout, steelhead, and some bass. Small spinners suit trout; larger sizes may suit bigger fish where allowed.

Spoons

Curved metal wobbles or flashes on retrieve. Cast them or troll them; vary speed to find a productive action.

Good for: trout, kokanee, salmon, and steelhead, with size and presentation adjusted for the water.

Crankbaits and hard baits

Hard-bodied lures wobble, dive, or float. Their diving bill and retrieve control how deep they run.

Good for: bass, trout, and other predatory fish. Match their running depth to water depth and cover.

Jigs and soft plastics

A weighted jig head carries a hook; soft-plastic bodies add shape and movement. Fish them on bottom, through cover, or with a swimming retrieve.

Good for: bass, crappie, trout, and other species depending on size, color, and presentation.

Flies

Dry flies float, nymphs imitate underwater insects, and streamers imitate small fish or leeches. Fly fishing uses weighted fly line to cast these light patterns.

Good for: trout and steelhead. Choose patterns that match local insects, baitfish, and water conditions.

Some waters restrict bait, hooks, or lure types. Confirm the rules for the specific water before fishing.
