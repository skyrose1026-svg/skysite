Terminal Tackle

Terminal tackle is the small hardware at the business end of your line. Each piece changes how bait or a lure is presented.

What each piece does

Hooks

Hold bait or a lure and connect the fish to the line. Hook size should match the bait and target fish; a bigger hook is not automatically better. Remove or replace bent and rusty hooks.

Used for: bait fishing, soft plastics, and fly or lure rigs. Follow water-specific barbless or hook restrictions.

Bobbers / floats

Keep bait suspended at a set depth and show a bite. Adjust the depth so bait sits near where fish are feeding without dragging along the bottom.

Used for: trout, panfish, and river presentations such as a float-and-jig setup.

Weights and sinkers

Help cast and take bait or a lure down to depth. Use only enough weight to reach the intended zone; current and depth determine how much is needed.

Used for: bottom rigs, drift fishing, and some bait presentations. Check for local restrictions on sinker materials.

Swivels and snaps

A swivel can reduce line twist; a snap makes changing lures easier. Extra hardware can affect a lure’s action, so use the smallest practical size.

Used for: spinning lures, spoons, and rigs that tend to twist line.

Leaders

A short section between main line and hook or lure. Leaders can add abrasion resistance, change visibility, or make a fly setup easier to rig.

Used for: trout in clear water, bass around cover, and fly fishing. Choose material and strength to suit the fish and main line.

Hook and tackle rules vary by water. Check current ODFW regulations.
