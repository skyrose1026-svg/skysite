Fishing Rods

A rod casts the lure or bait, helps control the line, and cushions a fish’s runs. Choose its style to match the reel, then match length, power, and action to your target and fishing spot.

Rod styles

Spinning rods

Designed for spinning reels mounted below the rod. Easy to learn and versatile with light lures, bait, and floats.

Good for: stocked trout, panfish, bass, and general bank or boat fishing.

Casting rods

Designed for baitcasting reels mounted above the rod. Their trigger grip and guides suit heavier lures and accurate casts.

Good for: bass fishing around weeds or cover, and heavier lures. They take practice to cast without line overruns.

Fly rods

Built to cast weighted fly line, which carries a lightweight fly. Rod weight (such as 3-weight or 5-weight) should match the fly line and reel.

Good for: trout and steelhead with flies. A 3–5 weight is common for trout; larger fish and heavier flies call for a stronger setup.

Length, power, and action

Rod choiceWhat it meansCommon use
5–6 ft, ultralight/lightShort, light rod for small lures and delicate presentations.Bluegill, crappie, stocked trout on small ponds or tight streams.
6–7 ft, light/mediumVersatile cast distance and control; handles many light-to-medium lures.Trout, kokanee, bass, and general freshwater fishing.
7–9 ft, medium/medium-heavyLonger casts and more leverage; power for current or larger lures.Steelhead, salmon, bass in cover, and larger rivers.
Fast actionBends mostly near the tip for quick response and hook-setting.Jigs, soft plastics, and many all-purpose setups.
Moderate actionBends farther down the blank, helping cast and keep treble-hook lures pinned.Crankbaits, spoons, and moving lures.

Match the rod’s printed line and lure ratings. Rod lengths and powers are not standardized across brands, so use the ratings printed on the rod as your guide.
