Smallmouth Bass

Photo: Wikimedia Commons

The bronze bomber of moving water. The Rogue River's smallmouth fishery is one of the best in the Northwest hundreds of fish a day is realistic in summer.

Where to find them

Approximate locations only. Check local access and current regulations before fishing.

  • Rogue River (mainstem) world-class smallmouth from Grants Pass down; float and wade water everywhere
  • Umpqua River Oregon's famous smallmouth river, just north
  • Applegate River (lower) small numbers of smallmouth

What they eat and the lures that match

Prey When it matters Lure or bait that mimics it
Crawfish their staple All year Crawfish-colored crankbaits, tube jigs, grubs
Baitfish and fry Summer Small swimbaits, spinners, topwater walkers
Aquatic insects Early season Small jigs and flies

When they're most active

Season Best time Why
Summer (JuneSept) Morning and evening Topwater bite at first light; the most consistent season
Spring Midday warm-ups Fish move shallow as water passes 55F
Fall Afternoon Aggressive feeding before winter

Regulations

Always verify before you go. Seasons, bag limits, and gear rules change by water and time of year. This page is a guide, not the law check the official ODFW regulations for the water you're fishing.

Rule Typical baseline
License Required for anglers 12 and older
Daily limit Typically 5 bass per day in most waters check the water you fish
Rogue special rules Some Rogue sections encourage smallmouth harvest to protect salmon and steelhead check current regs

Gear basics

  • Medium spinning rod, 6 to 10 lb line
  • Small crankbaits and tube jigs
  • Topwater lures for summer mornings
  • Fly rod, 5 to 7 weight, with crawfish patterns

Fun facts

  • Smallmouth bass often use current and rocky structure rather than dense weeds.
  • Their bronze sides and vertical bars become more pronounced in some conditions.
