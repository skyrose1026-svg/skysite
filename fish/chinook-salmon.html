Chinook Salmon

Photo: Wikimedia Commons

Oregon's biggest salmon the king. The Rogue River's fall and spring chinook runs are legendary, and a fresh one is the fight of a lifetime.

Where to find them

Approximate locations only. Check local access and current regulations before fishing.

  • Rogue River (lower) fall run enters July through September; the bay and lower river fish first
  • Rogue River (middle and upper) fall run peaks SeptemberOctober; spring run MayJuly
  • Applegate River fall chinook spawning tributary
  • Chetco, Elk, and Sixes rivers south coast fall runs, best after rain

What they eat and the lures that match

Prey When it matters Lure or bait that mimics it
Baitfish (anchovies, herring) Ocean and estuary phase Anchovy baits, herring, plug-cut herring
Salmon eggs River phase Cured roe, egg patterns, artificial eggs
Reaction to lures All phases Kwikfish-style plugs, spinners, bobber-dogging bait

When they're most active

Season Best time Why
Fall run (SeptNov) Early morning Fish move on the tide and at first light
After freshets During rising water New water moves fish upstream and triggers bites
Spring run (MayJuly) Early morning, deep holes Springers hold in cold pools and bite best at dawn

Regulations

Always verify before you go. Seasons, bag limits, and gear rules change by water and time of year. This page is a guide, not the law check the official ODFW regulations for the water you're fishing.

Rule Typical baseline
License + Combined Angling Tag Salmon harvest requires the annual Combined Angling Tag
Seasons and quotas Set annually and can change in-season with run size check before every trip
Wild fish Many waters require releasing unclipped (wild) chinook
Gear rules Barbless hooks required on many salmon waters

Stocking

Cole Rivers Hatchery on the upper Rogue and coastal hatcheries raise chinook for the Rogue basin's runs.

Gear basics

  • Heavy rod, 8.5 to 10 feet
  • 20 to 30 lb line
  • Roe setup: cured eggs, bobber or drift rig
  • Plug setup: Kwikfish or Mag Lip style lure

Fun facts

  • Chinook are the largest Pacific salmon species.
  • Their dark gums help distinguish them from some other salmon.
