Black Rockfish

Photo: Wikimedia Commons

A common nearshore rockfish of Oregon's rocky reefs and kelp habitat. Rockfish includes many species; identify each fish and check current depth, season, and bag rules.

How to identify

  • Dark charcoal to black body, often lighter underneath.
  • Spiny dorsal fin and large eye.
  • Black rockfish are rockfish, not true bass; similar-looking species need careful identification.

Where to find them

Approximate locations only. Check local access and current regulations before fishing.

  • Brookings and Port Orford: nearshore rocky habitat reached from coastal ports.
  • Charleston / Coos Bay: access to nearshore reef areas when conditions permit.

See the coastal access map for approximate port regions, not specific fishing marks.

When they feed

Black rockfish are caught around reef structure during safe nearshore weather windows. Use only enough weight to present gear safely near the bottom.

Regulations

Always verify before you go. Rockfish species can have different limits. Follow current depth restrictions and descending-device rules, and avoid marine reserves and closed areas.

Check current ODFW regulations.

Stocking

Black rockfish are wild marine fish and are not stocked.

Gear basics

  • Medium saltwater rod with a fast-action tip.
  • Corrosion-resistant spinning or conventional reel.
  • Small metal jigs or bait rigs with weight matched to current.
  • Carry and know how to use a descending device where required.

Fun facts

  • Black rockfish can live for several decades.
  • Like many rockfish, they give birth to live young rather than laying eggs.
