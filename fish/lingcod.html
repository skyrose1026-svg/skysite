Lingcod

Photo: Wikimedia Commons

A large-mouthed reef predator found along Oregon's rocky coast. Lingcod fishing is usually from a boat near rocky structure; seasons, depth rules, and retention limits change.

How to identify

  • Long body, broad head, and a large mouth with prominent teeth.
  • Mottled green, brown, or gray coloring that blends with rocky reefs.
  • A long dorsal fin runs along much of the back.

Where to find them

Approximate locations only. Check local access and current regulations before fishing.

  • Port Orford Reef region: rocky offshore habitat; access is weather- and season-dependent.
  • Brookings and Charleston: ports serving nearshore reef grounds.

Markers on the coastal access map show port regions, not exact fishing locations.

When they feed

Check the current marine fishing calendar and fish only when swell, wind, and bar conditions are safe. Reef habitat can have strong current and rough seas.

Regulations

Always verify before you go. Open dates, depth limits, size rules, and bag limits can change. Identify the fish before retaining it and avoid closed areas and marine reserves.

Check current ODFW regulations.

Stocking

Lingcod are a wild marine species and are not stocked.

Gear basics

  • Medium-heavy to heavy saltwater rod and a corrosion-resistant reel.
  • Braided line and a strong leader for rocky structure.
  • Large swimbaits, metal jigs, or other lures matched to current rules.
  • Carry a PFD and ocean-safety equipment on every boat trip.

Fun facts

  • Lingcod are not true cod; they belong to the greenling family.
  • Some lingcod have naturally blue-green flesh that turns white when cooked.
