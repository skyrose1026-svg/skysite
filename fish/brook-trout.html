Brook Trout

Photo: Wikimedia Commons

A char, not a trout look for the white leading edge on the fins and the worm-track marbling on the back. Beautiful, aggressive, and perfect for high mountain lakes.

Where to find them

Approximate locations only. Check local access and current regulations before fishing.

  • High Cascade lakes Sky Lakes Wilderness lakes hold brook trout
  • Small mountain streams some headwater streams have self-sustaining fish

What they eat and the lures that match

Prey When it matters Lure or bait that mimics it
Aquatic and terrestrial insects All day in cold water Small spinners, dry flies, worms
Small fish Larger brook trout Small spoons and streamers

When they're most active

Season Best time Why
Summer Mid-morning to afternoon High lakes warm just enough to trigger feeding
Fall Midday Fish feed heavily before winter; colors are at their best

Regulations

Always verify before you go. Seasons, bag limits, and gear rules change by water and time of year. This page is a guide, not the law check the official ODFW regulations for the water you're fishing.

Rule Typical baseline
License Required for anglers 12 and older
Limits Often generous brook trout can overpopulate small water, and ODFW encourages harvest in some lakes

Gear basics

  • Small spinners and flies
  • Worms for bait waters

Fun facts

  • Brook trout are char, not true trout.
  • Their fins have distinctive white front edges, often followed by a dark line.
