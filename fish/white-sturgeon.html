White Sturgeon

Photo: Wikimedia Commons

A long-lived native fish of Oregon rivers and estuaries. White sturgeon are found mainly in the Columbia River system; check the current rules for the exact area before fishing.

How to identify

  • Long, shark-like body with five rows of hard bony scutes.
  • Four barbels sit in front of the underslung mouth.
  • The upper lobe of the tail is longer than the lower lobe.

Where to find them

White sturgeon are associated with large rivers and estuaries. These markers show broad river regions, not exact fishing spots.

  • Columbia River: the main Oregon sturgeon fishery, with rules that differ by river section.
  • Lower Willamette River: sturgeon occur in the lower river; check local regulations and access.

When they feed

Sturgeon may feed throughout the year, but water level, current, and seasonal movement affect where they hold. Match the rig to the current and fish only where conditions are safe.

Regulations

Always verify before you go. Sturgeon seasons, size rules, and retention allowances vary by river section and can change. Do not keep a fish unless current rules explicitly allow it.

Check current ODFW regulations.

Stocking

White sturgeon are native and naturally reproduce; they are not a routine hatchery-stocked sport fish.

Gear basics

  • Heavy conventional rod and reel matched to the current and fish size.
  • Strong main line and an abrasion-resistant leader.
  • Use legal tackle and bait for the exact river section.
  • Plan for quick release and keep fish in the water when possible.

Fun facts

  • White sturgeon are among the largest freshwater fish in North America.
  • Their skeleton is mostly cartilage, and rows of bony scutes protect their body.
