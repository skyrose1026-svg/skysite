Pacific Halibut

Photo: Wikimedia Commons

A powerful flatfish sought offshore from Oregon ports. Halibut seasons, quotas, and open areas vary by year, so check both ODFW and NOAA before booking a trip.

How to identify

  • Flattened, oval body with both eyes on the dark upper side.
  • Mottled brown upper side and pale underside.
  • Large mouth and a crescent-shaped tail.

Where to find them

Approximate locations only. Check local access and current regulations before fishing.

Pacific halibut are caught offshore. These are port regions and launch areas, not offshore fishing coordinates; use current charts, forecasts, and local guidance.

  • Charleston / Coos Bay: ocean trips depart when seasons and conditions allow.
  • Port Orford and Brookings: southern coast ports serving offshore grounds.

When they feed

Plan around the published opening and safe weather windows. Tides and current affect whether you can present gear near bottom; season dates and quotas are set by fishery managers.

Regulations

Always verify before you go. Open dates, quotas, areas, and limits change. Follow the current Oregon and federal rules for the exact area and trip.

ODFW regulations · NOAA Pacific halibut species and management information

Stocking

Pacific halibut are a wild marine species and are not stocked.

Gear basics

  • Heavy conventional rod and high-capacity reel.
  • Braided main line and an abrasion-resistant leader.
  • Heavy jigs or lead sized for the depth and current.
  • Carry required safety gear and check ocean conditions before departure.

Fun facts

  • As a young halibut grows, one eye migrates to the other side of its head.
  • Halibut can change their upper-side coloring to better match the seafloor.
