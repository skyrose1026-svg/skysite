Cabezon

Photo: Wikimedia Commons

A large-headed bottom fish that blends into rocky Oregon reef habitat. Cabezon are often caught while targeting rockfish, but their seasons and rules are distinct.

How to identify

  • Broad, heavy head with fleshy cirri above the eyes.
  • Mottled coloring varies from green and brown to red.
  • Spiny dorsal fin and a bottom-dwelling body shape.

Where to find them

Approximate locations only. Check local access and current regulations before fishing.

  • Port Orford and Brookings: rocky nearshore habitat accessed from local ports.
  • Charleston / Coos Bay: reef grounds are reached by boat when ocean conditions allow.

See the coastal access map for approximate port regions, not specific fishing marks.

When they feed

Fish near rocky bottom structure when seasons are open and ocean conditions are safe. Changing current can affect whether gear can be presented near bottom.

Regulations

Always verify before you go. Cabezon rules are species-specific; do not assume rockfish limits apply. Check current season, size, and bag rules, and avoid closed areas.

Check current ODFW regulations.

Stocking

Cabezon are wild marine fish and are not stocked.

Gear basics

  • Medium saltwater rod and a corrosion-resistant reel.
  • Braided line and a strong leader for reef structure.
  • Crab imitations, small jigs, or legal bait rigs.
  • Check ocean and bar conditions before launching.

Fun facts

  • Cabezon can lay bright blue-green eggs.
  • Despite their camouflage and large heads, cabezon are ambush predators.
