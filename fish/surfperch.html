Surfperch

Photo: Wikimedia Commons (rainbow surfperch shown; Oregon beaches also hold other surfperch species)

Surfperch is a family of nearshore fish that includes redtail and silver surfperch. They offer accessible beach and jetty fishing along the Oregon coast; identify the species and check local rules.

How to identify

  • Deep, laterally compressed body with a small mouth.
  • Color and markings vary by species; redtail surfperch have a reddish tail.
  • Surfperch are live-bearing fish: females give birth to developed young.

Where to find them

Approximate locations only. Check local access and current regulations before fishing.

  • Brookings beaches and harbor: look for safe surf conditions and public access.
  • Gold Beach and Bandon: sandy beaches and estuary edges can hold surfperch.
  • Coos Bay area: public shoreline access varies by location.

See the coastal access map for approximate regions. Markers are not precise fishing locations.

When they feed

Moving water can stir up prey in the surf zone. Check tide and surf forecasts, fish in daylight when possible, and never enter dangerous surf to reach a spot.

Regulations

Always verify before you go. Identify the surfperch species and check current marine bag limits. Use public access points and respect closures and private property.

Check current ODFW regulations.

Stocking

Surfperch are wild marine fish and are not stocked.

Gear basics

  • Light to medium spinning rod, 9 to 11 feet for beach casting.
  • Spinning reel with braided line and a leader.
  • Small surfperch rig or jigs, following current hook rules.
  • Watch for sneaker waves and strong currents; wear suitable safety gear.

Fun facts

  • Surfperch give birth to live young, which is uncommon among fish.
  • Many surfperch feed in nearshore water where waves stir up small prey.
