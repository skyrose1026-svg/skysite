Bull Trout

Photo: Wikimedia Commons

Oregon's largest native char — and a threatened species. There is no bull trout fishery in Oregon: if you catch one, it must be released immediately, unharmed.

Where to find them

  • Klamath basin tributaries — small reintroduced populations in cold headwater streams
  • Upper Rogue tributaries — rare; know how to identify one before fishing trout water

What they eat — and the lures that match

Regulations

Always verify before you go. Seasons, bag limits, and gear rules change by water and time of year. This page is a guide, not the law — check the official ODFW regulations for the water you're fishing.

Rule Typical baseline
Threatened species It is illegal to harvest or intentionally target bull trout in Oregon
If caught Release immediately with minimal handling; keep the fish in the water
Report sightings ODFW asks anglers to report bull trout catches — it helps recovery

Stocking

ODFW runs reintroduction programs, notably in the Klamath basin. Populations are closely monitored.

Gear basics

  • Know the identification: large char, pale yellow-orange spots, no black spots on the back fin
  • Barbless hooks help with safe release
