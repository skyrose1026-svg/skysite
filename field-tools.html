Knot & rigging guide

Palomar knot

Mono · fluorocarbon · braid | hooks, swivels, and lures

  1. Double about 6 inches of line and pass the loop through the eye.
  2. Tie a loose overhand knot with the doubled line.
  3. Pass the hook or lure through the large loop.
  4. Moisten, pull both standing line and tag end evenly, then trim.

Keep the loop open and away from sharp points while tightening.

Improved Clinch knot

Mono · fluorocarbon | small hooks and terminal tackle

  1. Pass the tag end through the eye and wrap it around the standing line 5–7 times.
  2. Pass the tag through the small loop beside the eye.
  3. Pass it back through the larger loop just formed.
  4. Moisten, pull the standing line to seat the coils, and trim the tag.

Use fewer wraps with thicker line; check that coils lie neatly before loading.

Uni knot

Mono · fluorocarbon · braid | hooks, swivels, and line connections

  1. Pass the line through the eye and double back to form a loop alongside the standing line.
  2. Wrap the tag around both parallel lines and through the loop 5–7 times.
  3. Moisten and pull the tag to snug the coils together.
  4. Slide the knot down to the eye; pull the standing line firmly and trim.

For joining two lines, tie a Uni knot with each line and draw the knots together.

Non-slip loop knot

Mono · fluorocarbon | lures that benefit from free movement

  1. Make a loose overhand knot several inches from the tag end.
  2. Pass the tag through the lure eye, then back through the overhand knot.
  3. Wrap the tag around the standing line 4–5 times.
  4. Pass the tag back through the overhand knot the same way it entered.
  5. Moisten, tighten gradually, and verify the loop stays open at the eye.

Knot diagrams vary; practice with spare line and test the connection before fishing.
